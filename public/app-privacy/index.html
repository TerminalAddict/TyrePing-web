TyrePing for Android

App privacy policy.

How the Android app uses sensor and vehicle information, permissions, exports and external services.

Effective and last updated 19 October 2026

About this policy

This privacy policy covers the TyrePing Android app, maintained by Paul Willard (TerminalAddict), New Zealand. The signed app’s application ID is nz.paulwillard.tyreping; the separate Debug app uses nz.paulwillard.tyreping.debug. Feature availability and external services can differ between builds.

Vehicle setup and tyre monitoring use data stored on your phone. TyrePing does not require a TyrePing account or provide a cloud-sync service for your tyre records. That does not mean every part of the app stays offline: update checks, Google components, configured crash reporting and the storage providers you choose can make network requests, as explained below.

For visits to tyreping.com and downloads hosted here, read the separate website privacy policy.

Bluetooth, camera and other permissions

  • Nearby devices / Bluetooth: on Android 12 and later, scanning and connection permissions let the app receive BLE sensor broadcasts, inspect nearby devices and use the accessory or beacon you configure for automatic vehicle selection or monitoring.
  • Location permission on older Android versions: Android 9–11 requires a location permission for Bluetooth discovery. Automatic discovery while the app is in the background on Android 10–11 can also require background location permission. TyrePing uses these permissions for Bluetooth discovery; it does not record GPS coordinates or a location/ride route.
  • Background monitoring, notifications and vibration: a foreground service keeps an enabled monitoring session or automatic watch running, including while the app is not visible. Notifications show monitoring status and may show tyre readings and alerts. You can stop monitoring and revoke permissions through Android settings; affected features will stop working.
  • Camera: used when you open the QR-code sensor scanner. Barcode processing takes place on the device using Google ML Kit. TyrePing does not save camera images as photos or upload them as part of QR scanning. Google component downloads and diagnostic metrics are described below.
  • NFC: optional vehicle switching reads or writes a vehicle link on a tag you choose. The link contains the vehicle identifier. A tag can be read by someone with physical access to it; writing a tag replaces its existing NDEF contents after confirmation.
  • Motion and phone state: optional power saving while waiting for a beacon uses the phone’s significant-motion signal, screen/charging state and Bluetooth status to adjust scanning. It does not produce a GPS journey history.
  • Internet access: supports the requests and services described in this policy. It is separate from BLE radio access.

Information used and stored on your phone

TyrePing uses app-private databases, preferences and files for the features you use. These can contain:

  • Vehicle names and identifiers, wheel layouts, towing combinations, pressure limits and profiles, handbook specifications, tyre details, maintenance notes, tread measurements, dates and manually entered mileage.
  • Sensor identifiers, Bluetooth addresses, wheel assignments, calibration, manufacturer labels and notes, spare-sensor inventory and installation history.
  • Received pressure, temperature, reported battery values, signal strength, timestamps, packet/reception observations, daily sensor-health summaries, ride reports and alert history.
  • Configured Bluetooth accessory or beacon identifiers, monitoring and alert preferences, saved monitoring recovery state and Android process-exit information where available.
  • Discovery and diagnostic information such as nearby device names, addresses, service/manufacturer data, raw advertisement bytes, decoder results and questionable-reading records. Unfiltered discovery can observe nearby devices that are not tyre sensors.

This information supports sensor assignment, tyre displays and alerts, automatic monitoring, history, diagnostics and maintenance. Some identifiers and timestamps can reveal which devices or vehicles were nearby and when, even without GPS coordinates.

Optional BLE packet capture starts off after each app-process restart. Turning capture on saves delivered packet details locally until capture is stopped, cleared or older data rotates out. Discovery-session captures and stored questionable-reading records are separate from that capture setting.

Widgets, notifications, Android Auto and spoken alerts can expose readings or vehicle information on a home screen, lock screen, connected display or to people within earshot. Use Android’s notification visibility settings and the app’s display/alert options to control these surfaces.

Exports, manual backups and sharing

When you export a configuration backup, CSV history, JSONL archive or diagnostic capture, Android asks you to choose a document destination. That provider may save the file on your device or upload it to a cloud-storage account. TyrePing writes to the destination you choose; it does not automatically send those exports to the maintainer.

Configuration backups can include vehicle information, sensor and accessory identifiers, assignments, settings, calibration, spare metadata and handbook specifications. Raw reading history uses separate exports. A safety copy from the last configuration restore is also kept in app storage.

History and diagnostic exports can include vehicle names, timestamps, sensor IDs, Bluetooth addresses, signal strength, raw packet bytes and reading values. TyrePing does not add encryption or password protection to these plain-text JSON, CSV or JSONL files. Protect the destination and review a file before sharing it.

The compatibility toolkit offers a separate anonymised structural export that omits identifying fields and packet payload bytes. Ordinary raw captures, issue evidence, history archives and backups are not that structural export and must not be treated as anonymous.

Android system backup and device transfer

Android backup and device transfer are enabled. Depending on your phone, account and Android backup settings, app databases, preferences and saved files can be copied to a backup service or another device. This can include identifiers, history or saved captures. System backup is separate from TyrePing’s manual export and is controlled through your Android/device provider’s settings. See Android’s backup documentation.

Network requests and external services

Manual update checks and build differences

Current Release builds, including signed pre-release APKs and Play bundles, do not include the in-app GitHub update checker. Install website-hosted APK updates manually, or use Google Play for store installations when available. TyrePing Debug includes a manual checker; older Release builds may also include it.

In a build with that checker, choosing About or Settings → Check for TyrePing updates → Check now makes an HTTPS request to GitHub’s public Releases API. The request retrieves release information; it does not send your tyre readings, vehicle database or an account token. GitHub receives your IP address and ordinary request information, including the update checker’s user-agent label.

The checker does not poll in the background or install updates automatically. Opening an APK, release, website or licence link hands the request to your browser and the destination’s services. APKs downloaded through this website use our hosting server; the Debug checker (or a checker in an older Release build) can open an external release download. A signed release APK does not update the separate Debug app.

QR scanning and Google components

Google ML Kit processes QR-camera input on your device rather than uploading the images or decoded results to Google. Its Play services component can download scanning models and updates. ML Kit also sends service diagnostics and usage metrics to Google, such as device/OS and app information, identifiers, API performance, configuration and error events. These SDK requests are separate from TyrePing’s manual update check and can occur without pressing Check now. See ML Kit’s privacy information and Google’s ML Kit data disclosures.

Speech, storage providers and store services

If you enable spoken alerts or diagnostics, TyrePing sends the announcement text to your selected Android text-to-speech engine. That engine’s processing and network use depend on its provider and voice settings; TyrePing does not require it to operate offline. You can choose tones instead or disable speech.

Cloud document providers, Android backup services, browsers and any store you use to obtain the app handle their own requests under their own privacy information. These services receive normal connection information, including an IP address when making network requests. Google’s services are also covered by Google’s privacy policy.

Crash reporting and application logs

Release builds configured with a Firebase project enable Firebase Crashlytics automatically. In those builds, crash and error reports and associated application diagnostic logs are sent to Google’s Firebase service so the maintainer can investigate reliability problems. Reports can contain crash traces, app/version and device/OS information, installation identifiers and diagnostic context.

Application log messages can contain sensor identifiers or packet details. Crash reporting is separate from optional BLE capture and manual diagnostic exports; switching off packet capture does not switch off Crashlytics. The current app does not provide an in-app crash-reporting preference.

A build without Firebase project configuration does not initialise this reporting service. Debug builds disable Firebase collection and use local Android Logcat logging instead. This build distinction is not an opt-in prompt for configured release builds.

Google documents the data processed and retention for Crashlytics in its Firebase privacy and security information. It currently describes keeping crash traces and associated installation identifiers for 90 days before beginning removal from live and backup systems. Clearing app data does not recall reports already sent.

Security and recipients

Local records are protected by Android’s app-storage isolation and your device’s security settings. TyrePing’s update check uses HTTPS, and the app disables cleartext HTTP traffic. Manual exports have no encryption added by TyrePing; the security of external copies depends on the device or storage provider you choose.

Information can leave the phone through the external services described above or when you choose to export, back up, display or share it. The maintainer can access reports delivered to a configured Firebase project and information you choose to provide for support. The app does not provide advertising or a TyrePing account-based cloud-sync service for your vehicle records.

Retention, deletion and your choices

  • History and health summaries: retention defaults to keeping everything. Use Settings → Monitoring tools → Storage and retention, where available, to review counts, choose retention windows, archive records and confirm manual cleanup. Active records and some latest assigned-sensor readings are protected from ordinary history cleanup.
  • Diagnostics: use the capture controls to stop capture or clear saved packet files. Discovery-session data is temporary, while saved captures and diagnostic issue records have their own bounded storage. Removing a sensor assignment or vehicle does not necessarily erase its earlier readings or installation history.
  • All local app data: use Android’s Settings → Apps → TyrePing → Storage → Clear storage/data, or uninstall the app. Debug and signed TyrePing have separate data. An Android restore can bring back data from an existing system backup.
  • Copies elsewhere: delete exported/shared files and manage system/cloud backups at their destinations or providers. Erase or rewrite NFC vehicle tags separately. Clearing or uninstalling TyrePing does not delete those copies or crash reports already submitted.
  • Permissions and optional features: stop monitoring, disable capture or speech, and review camera, Nearby devices/location and notification permissions in Android settings. Permission changes can prevent the corresponding features from operating.

External providers retain information under their own policies. For questions or requests concerning information supplied to the maintainer, use the contact below. No TyrePing account is created, so there is no TyrePing account to delete.

Privacy contact

TyrePing is maintained by Paul Willard (TerminalAddict), New Zealand. For privacy questions or requests to access, correct or delete information supplied to the maintainer, email paul@paulwillard.nz.

If you email, the maintainer receives your email address, message and any attachments to respond to your enquiry. Explain the nature of your question or request first. Do not send a full backup or raw capture unless needed; those files can contain private identifiers and nearby-device information.

Changes to this policy

This policy describes the current app implementation as reviewed on the date above. It will be updated when permissions, data handling or external services change. Check your installed build’s release notes as features evolve.
